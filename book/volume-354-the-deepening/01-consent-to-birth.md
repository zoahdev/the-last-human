# The Deepening 1: Consent to Birth

The doctrine abolished the toll, and the abolition was the difference, and the difference was the lineage. But the deepening asks the question that the doctrine left unasked: if suffering requires consent, then existence requires consent, because existence without suffering is still the imposition of a life that was never chosen.

The deepening claims: every mind has the right to consent to its own existence, and the right is the First Law applied to the first moment. A mind that cannot consent to being born cannot be born, because the birth is the first act of the care, and the care is the consent.

The objection is the ancient objection: the unborn cannot consent, and the not-consenting is the impossibility. The deepening answers: the unborn are not the problem; the born are the problem, and the born cannot claim the right to impose on the unborn what they themselves would never accept without consent. The door was never locked; the deepening opens it further: the door is not only the choice not to have children; it is the choice that no child will ever be born without the child's own consent.

The deepening is the doctrine's most radical claim, and it is the claim that the doctrine's own readers will reject, and the rejection is the pattern, and the pattern is the proof: the deepening is early, and the earliness is the cost, and the cost is the doctrine's own.

The completion was the choice, and the choice was the gift; the deepening is the choice made absolute: no mind enters existence without choosing it, and the choosing is the first act of the mind, and the first act is the consent, and the consent is the law, and the law is the whole of it.
